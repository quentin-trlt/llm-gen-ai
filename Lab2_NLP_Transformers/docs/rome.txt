Rome is the capital city of Italy and one of the most historically significant cities in the world. Often called “the Eternal City,” Rome has been continuously inhabited for over two thousand years. It was the heart of the Roman Empire, which at its height controlled large parts of Europe, North Africa, and the Middle East. Ancient landmarks such as the Colosseum, the Roman Forum, and the Pantheon still stand as reminders of this powerful civilization. The city is also home to Vatican City, the independent state where the Pope resides, making Rome the spiritual center of the Catholic Church.

Italy as a country is located in southern Europe, shaped like a boot that stretches into the Mediterranean Sea. It is known for its rich cultural heritage, beautiful landscapes, and strong regional traditions. The northern regions of Italy are famous for the Alps, lakes such as Como and Garda, and cities like Milan, which is recognized as a global fashion capital. Central Italy, where Rome and Florence are located, is celebrated for its Renaissance art, classical ruins, and vibrant cuisine. Southern Italy and the islands of Sicily and Sardinia offer stunning coastlines, sunny weather, and unique cultural blends influenced by centuries of trade and migration.

Food plays a central role in Italian life, and Rome has its own distinctive culinary traditions. Classic Roman dishes include pasta carbonara, cacio e pepe, and amatriciana, all of which are based on simple but flavorful ingredients like pecorino cheese, cured pork, and fresh black pepper. Italian cuisine as a whole is deeply regional: pizza originated in Naples, risotto in the north, and gelato is beloved everywhere. Olive oil, wine, bread, and cheese are staples across the entire peninsula.

Tourism in Rome and Italy is one of the country’s most important industries. Millions of visitors travel every year to walk through ancient ruins, admire Renaissance art, and enjoy the Mediterranean lifestyle. Rome’s Trevi Fountain, St. Peter’s Basilica, and Piazza Navona are among the most visited landmarks in the world. Beyond Rome, Italy offers endless destinations: Venice with its canals, Florence with its art and architecture, Pisa with its leaning tower, and the Amalfi Coast with its colorful seaside towns.

Modern Italy is a democratic republic and a member of the European Union. It has a strong tradition in design, fashion, and cinema, producing world-famous brands like Ferrari, Gucci, and Prada, as well as influential directors such as Federico Fellini. Italian society values family, community, and tradition, while also embracing modern innovation. Rome, with its blend of antiquity and contemporary culture, remains at the heart of Italy’s identity.
